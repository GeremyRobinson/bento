# Brief: Adding fractions (`add`)

**Kits:** tape (bars) and fractions over one bottom. `sub` and `mix` use the same design.
**Status:** approved by G on 20 Oct 2026, built and tried. Also approved: the first and last steps' words, and a number in a fraction written as one word centred on its column.

## The idea
You can only add pieces of the same size. Once both bars are cut into eighths, you just count the eighths.

## The design
Each bar is its fraction drawn. It sits on the same rows as its fraction in the working, so the two read as one thing.
- The bars are on the left, as the picture is in Multiply.
- The working is on the right, and it's the only place the problem is written.
- What to do sits level with the box, and Check goes under the working.

**School working:** the vertical way 5th grade writes it.
- The two fractions are stacked with the sign.
- Each is rewritten over the new bottom after an `=`.
- A line goes under them, with the answer under the line.
- The simplest form follows the answer after an `=`.

## Steps
Each step lists what to do, where the box is, and what the picture does.

1. **"Look at the bars. Are the pieces the same size?"** There's no box. The bars are cut into the fractions' own pieces.
2. **"What size pieces fit both? Type the new bottom."** The box is the first rewritten fraction's bottom. When it's typed, both bars are cut into the new pieces and both new bottoms are written.
3. **"How many eighths is one half?"** The box is that fraction's new top. The bar's pieces are counted as the child looks. A fraction already over the new bottom is written for the child, so there's no step for it.
4. **"Add the tops."** or **"Take away the tops."** The box is the answer's top, and its bottom is given. The answer bar fills one piece after another, counted. Taking away crosses out the pieces taken.
5. **"Write it as simply as you can."** This step only comes when the answer can be simplified. There's a box for the whole number (when there is one) and boxes for the fraction. When the answer simplifies, the answer bar's cuts change to the simplest pieces (3 sixths becomes one half).
6. **"Same-size pieces, so the tops add."** The answer is in the working in the chapter colour.

## Scenarios
| | Example | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| A | 1/2 + 3/8 | One bottom fits the other: only one fraction is rewritten. |
| B | 1/4 + 1/6 | Both are rewritten. Bars are 24 squares long or less, and each piece is a whole number of squares. |
| C | 3/4 + 5/6 = 19/12 | The answer bar spills into a second bar. Simplify writes `1 7/12`. |
| D | 1/6 + 1/3 = 3/6 | Simplify writes `1/2`, and the answer bar's cuts change to halves. |
| E | a story | The story is told over the work. The sign is hidden until the child picks Add. |

## Decided by G (20 Oct)
- A fraction already over the new bottom is written for the child.
- When the answer is already as simple as it gets, there's no simplify step.
- The child types the new bottom. Cutting the bars by hand would be a new hand move, so it isn't used yet.

## Open
- A number in a fraction is written as one word centred on its column, so 12 reads as one number. It isn't two digits two squares apart, as in column working.
- The pieces show their count numbers only when a piece is at least 2 squares wide.
